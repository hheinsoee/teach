# Grading Policy

## The decision that defines this course

A student stands up on day 12. Their app is live, polished, and works perfectly.
You open a file and ask what it does. They cannot say.

**They do not pass.**

Decide this now, before the cohort starts, and say it to students in day 1.
Under pressure — a beautiful app, a proud student, a watching class — the temptation
to pass them is enormous. Every course that gives in stops meaning anything, and the
students who did the work find out their certificate is worth nothing.

## Pass requirement

All six rubric criteria. No partial passes on 2, 3, or 4.

## Remediation

A student failing only criteria 2, 3, or 4 gets **one** re-examination, no sooner
than one week later, on a different random file and a different broken-code sample.

They are not re-examined on the same file. The point is understanding, not memorizing
one answer.

No second remediation. Repeating the course is free for them, once.

## What is not a reason to pass

- The app looks good
- They worked hard
- They attended everything
- They were nervous
- They can explain a *different* file
- They would have known it with the agent open

## What is not a reason to fail

- Forgetting a keyword or syntax name
- Not knowing the framework internals
- Not knowing library source code
- A bug in production on demo day — ask them to diagnose it instead; that is a better exam

## Attendance

Missing two consecutive days means the student cannot catch up. Move them to the
next cohort. This is kinder than letting them drown through weeks 4–5.

## Expected distribution, per 10 students

| Outcome | Expected |
|---|---|
| Live, working app | 8 |
| Pass criteria 2, 3, 4 honestly | 5–6 |
| Drop out, usually week 4 | 2–3 |

If a cohort comes in at 9 or 10 passes, the oral exam has gone soft. Check it.
